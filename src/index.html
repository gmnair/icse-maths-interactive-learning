01 · COMMERCIAL MATHEMATICS

Maths for everyday money

2 topics

Goods and Services Tax

GST is calculated on the taxable value of an item. For an intra-state sale, the total GST is split equally into CGST and SGST; for an inter-state sale, the full rate is charged as IGST.

Tax = taxable value × GST rate ÷ 100
Final bill = taxable value + tax
Worked example · Find the bill after GST

A mixer costs ₹4,500 before GST. At 18%, tax is ₹4,500 × 18/100 = ₹810. For an intra-state purchase, CGST = ₹405 and SGST = ₹405. Amount payable = ₹5,310.

Recurring deposits

When equal instalments are deposited monthly, each instalment earns interest for a different length of time. For monthly instalment \(P\), \(n\) months and annual rate \(r\%\), the school-level formula is:

Interest = P × n(n + 1) × r ÷ 24 ÷ 100
Maturity value = Pn + interest
Worked example · Recurring deposit interest

₹1,000 is deposited each month for 12 months at 6% p.a. Interest = 1,000 × 12 × 13 × 6 ÷ (24 × 100) = ₹390. Maturity value = ₹12,000 + ₹390 = ₹12,390.

02 · ALGEBRA

Spot the structure, solve the problem

7 topics

Linear inequations

Solve as you would an equation, but reverse the inequality when multiplying or dividing by a negative number. Show the solution set on a number line.

−2x + 5 > 1 ⟹ −2x > −4 ⟹ x < 2

Quadratic equations

Write in standard form \(ax^2+bx+c=0\). Factorise where possible, or use the quadratic formula. The discriminant \(b^2-4ac\) indicates the nature of the roots.

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a

Ratio and proportion

In a proportion \(a:b=c:d\), the product of the extremes equals the product of the means: \(ad=bc\). Direct, inverse and continued proportions are useful in word problems.

a : b = c : d ⟹ ad = bc

Factor & remainder theorems

When \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x-a\), the remainder is \(f(a)\). Therefore \(x-a\) is a factor exactly when \(f(a)=0\).

Remainder on division by (x − a) = f(a)

Matrices

Add or subtract matrices of the same order entry by entry. For multiplication, the number of columns in the first matrix must equal the number of rows in the second. For a 2 × 2 matrix, use the determinant and inverse when the determinant is non-zero.

A = [a b; c d]   det(A) = ad − bc

Arithmetic progressions

An AP has a constant common difference \(d\). Use the nth term and sum formulae to find a term or the total of the first \(n\) terms.

tₙ = a + (n−1)d  ·  Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n−1)d]

Coordinate geometry

Use coordinates to describe points and line segments. The midpoint averages coordinates; the section formula divides a segment in a stated ratio. The slope helps write and compare straight-line equations.

Midpoint = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)
Slope m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)
Line: y = mx + c
WORKED EXAMPLE

Factorise and solve

Solve \(x^2-5x+6=0\). Find two numbers whose product is 6 and sum is −5: −2 and −3.

x² − 5x + 6 = (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0
∴ x = 2 or x = 3

03 · GEOMETRY

See the relationships. Prove the result.

3 topics

Similarity

Similar triangles have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides. The ratio of their areas is the square of the ratio of corresponding sides.

AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF
Area₁ / Area₂ = (side₁ / side₂)²

Circles

Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre. The perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord. Angles in the same segment are equal; the angle in a semicircle is a right angle. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference
Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°

Constructions

Use a ruler and compass to construct tangents from an external point, divide a line segment in a given ratio, or construct a triangle similar to a given triangle at a stated scale.

Construction = accurate steps + labelled figure
Tangent facts: the radius to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent; tangents drawn from the same external point are equal in length.
Proof idea · Why is the angle in a semicircle 90°?

Let \(AB\) be a diameter and \(C\) a point on the circle. The angle at the centre standing on arc \(AB\) is a straight angle, \(180°\). The angle at the circumference on the same arc is half the angle at the centre, so \(\angle ACB=90°\).

Turn 2D measurements into 3D answers

Solids & combinations
▯

Cylinder

Curved surface area \(=2πrh\)
Total surface area \(=2πr(h+r)\)
Volume \(=πr²h\)

△

Cone

Slant height \(l=√(r²+h²)\)
Curved surface area \(=πrl\)
Volume \(=⅓πr²h\)

●

Sphere

Surface area \(=4πr²\)
Volume \(=⁴⁄₃πr³\)

◐

Hemisphere

Curved surface area \(=2πr²\)
Total surface area \(=3πr²\)
Volume \(=⅔πr³\)

REMEMBER

For combined solids

Break the shape into familiar solids. Add or subtract volumes as needed. For exposed surface area, include only surfaces you can see—do not count joined faces. Keep all measurements in the same units and state your answer in square or cubic units.

Worked example · Volume of a cylinder

A cylinder with radius 3 cm and height 8 cm has volume \(πr²h=π×3²×8=72π\), approximately 226.19 cm³.

05 · TRIGONOMETRY

Use an angle to find the unreachable

Identities · Applications

Ratios and identities

For an acute angle in a right-angled triangle, name the sides relative to the angle before choosing a ratio. SOH-CAH-TOA is a quick reminder.

sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse
tan θ = opposite / adjacent

Know the standard angles and complementary-angle relationships. The fundamental identity follows from Pythagoras' theorem. Divide through by \(\cos²θ\) or \(\sin²θ\) to obtain the other standard forms.

θ:   0°    30°    45°    60°    90°
sin θ: 0   1/2   1/√2   √3/2   1
cos θ: 1   √3/2   1/√2   1/2   0
sin(90°−θ) = cos θ  ·  cos(90°−θ) = sin θ
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
1 + tan²θ = sec²θ  ·  1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ
Worked example · Evaluate without a calculator

If \(\sin θ=3/5\) for an acute angle, then \(\cos θ=√(1−9/25)=4/5\). Hence \(\tan θ=(3/5)/(4/5)=3/4.

Heights and distances

Draw a right triangle, mark the angle of elevation or depression, then choose a ratio using the known and unknown sides. Assume the ground is level unless stated otherwise.

tan θ = height / horizontal distance
height = distance × tan θ

06 · STATISTICS

Let the data tell its story

Grouped data · Graphs

Measures of central tendency

For grouped data, use the class mark \(x_i\) and frequency \(f_i\). The median class is the first class whose cumulative frequency reaches \(N/2\).

Mean = Σfᵢxᵢ / Σfᵢ
Median = l + [(N/2 − cf) / f] × h
Mode = l + [(f₁ − f₀) / (2f₁ − f₀ − f₂)] × h

In the median formula, \(l\) is the lower boundary of the median class, \(cf\) the cumulative frequency before it, \(f\) its frequency and \(h\) the class width. In the mode formula, \(f₁\) is modal-class frequency and \(f₀,f₂\) are the preceding and following frequencies.

Worked example · Find the grouped mean

For classes 0–10, 10–20, 20–30 with frequencies 2, 3, 5, the class marks are 5, 15, 25. Thus \(Σf_ix_i=10+45+125=180\) and \(N=10\). Mean = 180/10 = 18.

Histograms and ogives

Draw a histogram with adjoining bars; if class widths differ, use frequency density. A less-than ogive plots upper class boundaries against cumulative frequencies. It can help estimate the median and quartiles.

07 · PROBABILITY

Count the outcomes, understand the chance

Elementary events

The probability of an event

When all outcomes are equally likely, probability compares favourable outcomes with all possible outcomes. It always lies between 0 and 1.

P(E) = number of outcomes favourable to E
÷ total number of equally likely outcomes
P(not E) = 1 − P(E)
Worked example · A fair six-sided die

For a fair die, the outcomes are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. The event “roll a prime number” has outcomes 2, 3, 5, so \(P(prime)=3/6=1/2\).

For an experiment, relative frequency (successes divided by trials) estimates probability. More trials often make the observed proportion settle closer to the theoretical probability, but a short run can vary.
